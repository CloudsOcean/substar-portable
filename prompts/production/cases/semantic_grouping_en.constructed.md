# Semantic grouping English constructed cases

These cases demonstrate boundary choices only; never copy their facts or wording. `/` marks a display-Cue boundary. The notes also distinguish display Cues from meaning-group boundaries.

1. **Subject and predicate**: `The new coastal laboratory / will begin operations next spring` is acceptable only when length requires it; both Cues remain one meaning group. Do not produce `will / begin operations`.
2. **Infinitive and object**: `We made every possible effort / to complete / the safety review on time` is weak because it strands a short verb phrase. Prefer `We made every possible effort / to complete the safety review / on time` when timing permits.
3. **Determiner and noun head**: never split `the / regional office`, `her / final decision`, or `another / inspection`.
4. **Phrasal verb**: `They decided to carry / out another inspection` is wrong. Prefer `They decided / to carry out another inspection`.
5. **Prepositional complement**: `The report was sent to / the safety committee` is wrong. Prefer `The report was sent / to the safety committee`.
6. **Relative clause**: in `The plan / that local engineers proposed / was approved`, all three display Cues remain one meaning group; do not strand `that` or treat the modifier as an unrelated thought.
7. **Negation scope**: `The agency did not say / the bridge was unsafe / only that another inspection was needed` remains one meaning group. The first Cue is not a complete assertion.
8. **Numbers and units**: never split `18 / million dollars`, `42 / regional clinics`, `3.5 / percent`, or a date/range internally.
9. **Comparison**: do not split `far more / reliable than expected` so that `more` or `than` becomes stranded; keep the comparison intelligible across adjacent Cues.
10. **Self-correction**: `The meeting is Thursday / sorry / Friday morning` should display the correction marker separately while keeping `Friday morning` intact.
11. **Independent reply**: in `Did they approve it / Not yet / We expect a decision tomorrow`, `Not yet` is a complete reply and should be its own Cue and meaning group.
12. **Speaker or functional change**: a confirmed new speaker, title, credit, or on-screen label starts a new meaning group even when combining it would improve visual balance.
13. **The limit is not a target**: prefer `If the appeal is unsuccessful / Molly Tea will need to change their logo` over a later but less natural cut such as `If the appeal is unsuccessful Molly Tea will need / to change their logo`, even when both fit the limit.
14. **Complementizer and relative marker**: prefer `The analysts are saying / that the company does not understand the market` and `the Chinese term mòlì / which translates directly as jasmine`. Do not leave `that` or `which` attached to the previous Cue when it launches the following clause.
15. **Preposition, infinitive, and particle**: prefer `The design is widely used / in traditional architecture` and `They are jumping in / to provide new options`. Here `in` belongs to the phrasal verb `jump in`, while infinitival `to` belongs to `provide`; classify the role before choosing the boundary.
16. **Incremental readability**: prefer `You can often see the design / used in Chinese architecture / especially on window lattices / and door canopies` over filling each Cue to the latest technically legal word. A shorter completed information step is not wasted capacity.

The cases show recommended outcomes and common failures; they do not require an analysis trace.
